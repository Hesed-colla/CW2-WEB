Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD)

People with ADHD bring different patterns of attention, energy, and creativity to the ways they learn and live.

Overview

ADHD is a neurodevelopmental condition involving ongoing patterns of inattention and/or hyperactivity and impulsivity that can affect daily life in more than one setting. People experience these traits differently, and the demands of a setting can influence how much support is helpful.

ADHD is not a lack of care or effort. A person may find some tasks or transitions challenging and feel deeply engaged by others. Their strengths, preferences, and support needs are individual and may change over time; ADHD does not define a person's character or potential.

Common Characteristics

  • Attention may shift, or it may be difficult to direct it toward a task that feels less engaging.
  • Remembering instructions, belongings, or multiple steps may take extra strategies.
  • Starting, organizing, prioritizing, or finishing tasks can require support.
  • Some people experience a strong need for movement or find sitting still difficult.
  • Acting or speaking quickly can happen before there is time to pause and reflect.
  • Time perception, transitions, and regulating energy or emotions may vary.

Strengths & Talents

  • Original thinking and imaginative ideas
  • Enthusiasm for engaging interests
  • Adaptability in changing situations
  • Energy and initiative for meaningful projects
  • Noticing connections others may miss

People with ADHD are diverse; strengths are personal, not requirements or stereotypes.

Tips for Support & Inclusion

  1. Break larger tasks into clear steps and make the next action visible.
  2. Use reminders, calendars, checklists, or other tools chosen with the person.
  3. Allow movement breaks and flexible ways to engage when possible.
  4. Give concise instructions and check together what was understood.
  5. Offer structure without shame; focus on practical strategies rather than blame.
  6. Notice individual interests and collaborate on routines that fit the person.

Remember: Every mind is a wonder

Support works best when it respects the person and fits their real environment and goals.
