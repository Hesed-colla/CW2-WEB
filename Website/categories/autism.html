Autism Spectrum Disorder

Autistic people experience and engage with the world in many different ways, each with their own perspectives, interests, and strengths.

Overview

Autism is a neurodevelopmental variation associated with differences in social communication and interaction, sensory processing, and patterns of interests or behavior. It is a spectrum: people have different combinations of traits and may need different kinds or levels of support in different settings.

Some autistic people communicate by speaking, while others use writing, sign, communication devices, or other methods. Sensory experiences, routines, and social expectations can affect how a person feels and participates. Every autistic person is unique, and a diagnosis does not define their personality, goals, or potential.

Common Characteristics

  • Communicating or interpreting social cues in ways that may differ from common expectations.
  • Strong interests, deep knowledge, or focused attention on particular subjects.
  • Finding predictability and clear routines helpful, or feeling unsettled by unexpected changes.
  • Experiencing sounds, lights, textures, movement, or other sensory input differently.
  • Using repeated movements or actions for expression, focus, or regulation.
  • Preferring direct language, extra processing time, or alternative communication methods.

Strengths & Talents

  • Careful attention to detail
  • Deep interest and expertise
  • Creative, original perspectives
  • Honesty and direct communication
  • Persistence with meaningful goals

These are possibilities, not expectations; autistic people are individuals with varied talents and preferences.

Tips for Support & Inclusion

  1. Ask the person how they prefer to communicate and what support feels useful.
  2. Use clear language, allow processing time, and share important information in writing when helpful.
  3. Offer predictable routines and advance notice of changes where possible.
  4. Consider sensory access, including quieter spaces and options for breaks.
  5. Respect stimming, communication devices, and other self-regulation strategies.
  6. Build on interests without assuming every autistic person shares the same abilities or needs.

Remember: Every mind is a wonder

Autistic people deserve respect, choice, access, and the opportunity to be known as themselves.
