Physical Disabilities

Accessible environments and respectful support help people move, participate, and make choices in ways that work for them.

Overview

Physical disability is a broad term for experiences that affect movement, strength, coordination, stamina, or physical access. Some people use mobility aids or other equipment; others do not. The disability may be present from birth, acquired later, or fluctuate over time.

Barriers in buildings, transportation, communication, or assumptions can have as much effect on participation as a person's physical condition. People have different preferences and support needs. Ask before helping, respect personal space and equipment, and remember that every person is more than how they move.

Common Characteristics

  • Movement, coordination, balance, or strength may differ or need additional effort.
  • Some people use wheelchairs, walkers, canes, braces, or other mobility equipment.
  • Fatigue, pain, or stamina can affect participation and may vary over time.
  • Some activities or environments may require additional time or an alternative way to participate.
  • Physical access needs can include entrances, routes, seating, restrooms, and transportation.
  • People may have physical access needs alongside other disabilities or health conditions.

Strengths & Talents

  • Skills, interests, and expertise in any field
  • Resourcefulness in navigating environments
  • Creativity and flexible problem-solving
  • Leadership and community knowledge
  • Determination toward personally meaningful goals

There is no single personality or set of talents associated with physical disability.

Tips for Support & Inclusion

  1. Ask before offering assistance and follow the person's instructions.
  2. Never move a mobility aid, wheelchair, or other equipment without permission.
  3. Keep routes clear and check that entrances, seating, and facilities are accessible.
  4. Plan enough time and offer flexible ways to take part in activities.
  5. Speak to the person directly, not only to a companion or support person.
  6. Do not assume that a person wants help or cannot participate; ask what works.

Remember: Every mind is a wonder

Access is a shared responsibility: ask, listen, and remove barriers together.
