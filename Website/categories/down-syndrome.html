Down Syndrome

People with Down syndrome bring their own interests, relationships, voices, and contributions to every community.

Overview

Down syndrome is a genetic condition in which a person has an extra copy or part of a copy of chromosome 21. It can influence development and may be associated with certain health considerations, but it does not determine a person's abilities, personality, or future.

People with Down syndrome have varied communication styles, learning profiles, interests, and support needs. Some may benefit from help with particular tasks or health care, while others need different supports in different settings. Every person is unique, and it is important to speak to and about each person with respect.

Common Characteristics

  • Developmental milestones may be reached on an individual timeline.
  • Learning may benefit from clear steps, repetition, and hands-on practice.
  • Speech and language development can vary; people may use multiple communication approaches.
  • Some people experience differences in muscle tone or motor development.
  • Health needs vary, and some people benefit from ongoing individualized health care.
  • Social interests, preferences, and ways of expressing emotion vary from person to person.

Strengths & Talents

  • Distinct interests and personal expertise
  • Creative expression through art, music, or play
  • Warm relationships and community connections
  • Persistence when pursuing meaningful goals
  • Unique perspectives and ways of solving problems

Strengths are individual; avoid assuming what someone can do based on a diagnosis.

Tips for Support & Inclusion

  1. Speak directly to the person and include them in conversations and decisions.
  2. Use age-respectful language, materials, and expectations.
  3. Give clear instructions in manageable steps and allow time to respond.
  4. Offer communication options such as visuals, gestures, or assistive tools when helpful.
  5. Make participation and learning opportunities accessible without lowering the person's right to meaningful challenge.
  6. Follow the person's and family's preferences and coordinate with chosen support professionals as appropriate.

Remember: Every mind is a wonder

A diagnosis is one part of a person’s story, never a measure of their worth or potential.
