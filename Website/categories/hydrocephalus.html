Hydrocephalus

Every child is more than a health condition. Learning about access and support helps children take part in the ways that work for them.

Overview

Hydrocephalus is a condition in which cerebrospinal fluid—the fluid that surrounds and cushions the brain and spinal cord—builds up in or around the brain. This can increase pressure and may affect a person's health and development. Hydrocephalus can be present from birth or develop later, and each person's experience is different.

Some people have medical treatment, such as a shunt to move fluid or a procedure called endoscopic third ventriculostomy (ETV). A person's care plan is individual and belongs with their health team. Having hydrocephalus does not tell us a child's personality, interests, or what they can learn.

Everyone's Experience Is Different

  • Health, movement, vision, learning, or energy needs can vary from person to person.
  • Some children may need regular appointments or support with medical equipment; others may have different routines.
  • Support needs can change over time, and not every child experiences the same effects.
  • A child's interests, friendships, ideas, and goals are part of who they are—not just their medical history.

The Whole Child Matters

  • Personal interests, favorite activities, and friendships
  • Ideas, questions, humor, and ways of communicating
  • Individual ways to participate and show what they know
  • Their own choices, hopes, and growing independence

These are not traits promised by a diagnosis; they are parts of getting to know each child as an individual.

Ways to Support a Child

  1. Ask the child and family what support is helpful instead of guessing.
  2. Work with the family's chosen school and health professionals on an individual plan.
  3. Make activities and spaces accessible, and offer flexible ways to join in.
  4. Respect privacy; let the child decide what they want to share with others.
  5. Be a good friend and classmate—include the child in games, conversation, and everyday choices.
  6. If the child has a care plan, follow the family's and health team's guidance.

Health and safety

Families should follow the child's health team's care plan. If a child with hydrocephalus or a shunt has new or worrying symptoms, tell a trusted adult and contact their healthcare team promptly. Sudden or severe symptoms need urgent medical attention.
